Be Ready

Disaster Preparedness Tips

Small steps today save families tomorrow. Start with the essentials.

Build a Go-Kit

  • Water: 3L per person, per day
  • Non-perishable food for 72 hours
  • Medicines, cash, and important documents
  • Flashlight, batteries, and a power bank

Plan Your Escape

  • Two exits from every room
  • A family meeting point outside
  • Nearby shelter location saved in your phone
  • Roles: who carries the kit, who walks the kids

Stay Connected

  • Save emergency numbers before you need them
  • Agree on an out-of-area contact
  • Charge devices when warnings are issued
  • Follow official alerts, not rumours

How We Respond

The Response, Step by Step

From the first alert to the last cup of tea.

Dispatch in Minutes

Alerts are verified by the incident desk, then crews are matched by skill and distance — average dispatch under 18 minutes.

Safety-First Fieldwork

Every deployment opens with a safety brief. Crews work in pairs, call out hazards, and stand down before anyone gets hurt.

Aftercare & Recovery

We hand over to shelter, aid, and recovery teams so no family is ever left holding a closed incident file.

In the Moment

What to Do When It Happens

Quick, proven moves for the first minutes of each crisis.

During a storm or flood

Move to the highest floor, away from windows. Never walk or drive through floodwater — six inches can carry a person and a foot can float a car. If your home is unsafe, move to solid ground and call the hotline.

During a wildfire

Leave early if an evacuation order is issued. Wear long sleeves and a mask or cloth, and drive with windows up. If trapped indoors, close every door and window and call for help.

During an earthquake

Drop, cover, and hold on until the shaking stops. Stay indoors until it does, then check for gas, fire, and damaged structures before exiting. Expect aftershocks.

During extreme heat

Stick to shade, drink water every 20 minutes, and never leave anyone in a parked car. Cool the body with wet cloths and check on older neighbours who live alone.
